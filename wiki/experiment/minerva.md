# MINERvA

Dedicated neutrino-nucleus cross-section experiment in the NuMI beam at Fermilab.
A fine-grained scintillator tracker with an upstream passive nuclear-target
region, read out in coincidence with the magnetized MINOS near detector as a
muon spectrometer (2503.15047).

## NuMI beam (2503.15047)

- Wide-band ν_μ beam: 120 GeV protons on a graphite target, two-horn focusing,
  675 m decay region (2503.15047).
- Medium-energy (ME) configuration: ⟨E_ν⟩ ∼ 6 GeV, neutrino-enhanced horn
  polarity with ∼3.8% ν̄ contamination (2503.15047).
- This analysis: 10.61 × 10²⁰ POT (2012–2019). MINERvA total ME accumulation
  quoted as 24.5 × 10²⁰ POT; the earlier low-energy (LE) run was 5.4 × 10²⁰ POT
  at ⟨E_ν⟩ ∼ 3 GeV (2503.15047).
- Flux: GEANT4 NuMI beamline simulation, hadron production reweighted to external
  data (MIPP, NA49) and constrained in situ by ν–e elastic scattering
  (2503.15047).

## Detector (2503.15047)

- 208 hexagonal planes, each 127 triangular polystyrene scintillator strips
  (≈1.7 cm high, ≈3.3 cm wide) in three (UVX) orientations for 3D tracking;
  overall length ≈5 m (2503.15047).
- **Nuclear-target region:** ≈1.25 m upstream region with passive C, Fe, Pb and
  water targets interleaved with scintillator planes — enabling simultaneous
  multi-nucleus measurement in one flux ([[c12]], [[fe56]], [[pb]], [[o16]]).
- **Tracker region:** 124 contiguous scintillator planes ([[ch]]) downstream of
  the nuclear targets (2503.15047).
- Downstream EM calorimeter (20 planes + 2 mm Pb) then hadronic calorimeter
  (20 planes + 2.54 cm steel) (2503.15047).
- **Muon spectrometer:** magnetized MINOS near detector, 2 m downstream, used as
  a muon range stack and charge/momentum spectrometer (sets the 2–20 GeV/c,
  <17° muon requirement) (2503.15047).
- Proton energy by range; the Bragg-peak end-of-track hit pattern flags
  inelastic/exiting protons and vetoes pions (2503.15047).

## Measurements at MINERvA in this wiki

- ν_μ CCQE-like ([[ccqe]]) A-dependence on [[c12]], [[ch]], [[o16]], [[fe56]],

  ⟨E_ν⟩ ∼ 6 GeV (2503.15047); source [[source/2503.15047]].
